A Handmade Web by J. R. Carpenter

The difference between the old web and the new web is that the new web has become unworkable. We're not quite passive observers (though our control is dwindling by the second), but we've lost the ability to build our own stages. Our expression mustn't occur outside the approved channels set out for us.

My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be? by Laurel Schwulst

A website can be a sticky note, an idea you scrawl down in a random burst of creative energy, promptly lose interest in and misplace, and then find months later and realize it's finally the right time to revisit.

A website is just another form of paper or clay or ink, it's just another medium for us to make stuff with. It may come with its own particular quirks, like any distinct medium, but we can do whatever we want with it. I can't wait to learn more and see what's possible with this new skill.

A Rant on Technology by Ursula K. Le Guin

To me, the difference between science fiction and fantasy fiction is only aesthetic - the words they use, the visuals they employ, the places they go, the names their characters have. Both are forms of fiction about people living in places different than our own, navigating their world's unique rules and technology. Whether that technology is wormholes and cybernetics or sorcerer's staves and siege engines is inconsequential. That's why I like the term speculative fiction, though that term is unfortunately still considered too dorky to be adopted by the general public.

Rant about popular fiction aside...

To relate this reading to our class, my takeaway is that creating a website using basic HTML and CSS isn't necessarily 'lower' than vibecoding using AI just because the latter method is more recent, more arcane, more 'advanced'.

Any time we adopt a new technology, we can't just think about what we gain from it, but what we're losing as well.
